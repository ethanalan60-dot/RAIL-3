The two printed estimands are generally different when \(m_h\) varies: the pooled mean weights each state by its eligible-pair count, whereas the state mean gives every included state equal weight. Equal pair counts are a sufficient condition for equality; unequal pair counts do not prove that the particular numerical outcomes differ. No historical or current metric has been recomputed to compare them. The unified main-text pooled equation is retained for the prospective results; reading the legacy printed definition does not authorize changing existing numbers. For the displayed legacy mean to be defined, each included state must have at least one eligible anchored non-STOP action. The printed formula does not establish which empty-set guard or reduction the historical execution actually used.

## C4. Decision metrics and denominator scope

Current normalized regret is the **ratio of summed selected regret to summed STOP regret** on the relevant complete-oracle population. STOP regret is oracle headroom because STOP belongs to the action set. The COCO quantity called utility improvement is `1 - normalized_regret`; its saved interval is dimensionless and cannot become a raw mismatch-gain or IoU interval. A zero oracle denominator remains undefined. These definitions are visible in DV-S01–03; none was reevaluated here.

Sparse gain capture divides the **signed** selected gain by the sum of the largest K nonnegative oracle gains on the same eligible population. FORCED_K uses exactly the registered capacity after frozen rounding and tie-breaking, including states whose predicted or realized gain is nonpositive. POSITIVE_GAIN_CAP may use fewer states but retains that K-specific oracle denominator. Both six-cell V6 meanings remain separate from COCO's primary FORCED_K binding and secondary-only cap; the original V6 and E4/E5 decision rules are not replaced by this crosswalk.

COCO's available fidelity cells and complete-oracle population are not interchangeable: a defined STOP/action pair can contribute to DRRE even when another feasible action's technical failure excludes that state from utility's oracle comparison. Pre-GT label blindness alone does not establish pre-action observability of a failed execution. The already documented offline availability-mask scope remains necessary; no deployment benefit is inferred from failure-conditioned offline selection.
